BLOOPS

Overview

BLOOPS was made during the 2025 Global Game Jam in 96 hours. It's a 3D top-down puzzle game where players redirect bubbles from a soap dispenser to a drain using objects that alter each bubble's trajectory.

This was my first game jam, on a multidisciplinary team of nine. I only knew a few people going in, including the other designer and one programmer I had worked with before. The team also included artists from DigiPen's Spain campus who had jam experience, and who mentioned that in past jams their work had never really been implemented the way they envisioned it.

Goal
Ship a working puzzle game in four days, and make sure the artists' work actually reached the build this time.

Making sure the art shipped

That comment from the Spain artists set my personal goal for the jam. Going in, I made it my job to make sure the gameplay assets actually got integrated and worked the way they were supposed to, because spending a weekend on art that never makes it into the build has to be a terrible feeling.

The other designer focused mainly on 2D elements like UI and menus, while I handled most of the 3D gameplay asset implementation. A lot of my time went into connecting assets to systems, setting up prefabs correctly, and confirming that what the artists made actually functioned in-game rather than just existing in the project.

Design and implementation

Once the theme was revealed I helped kick off brainstorming and made sure everyone's ideas got considered, so we landed on something the whole team was actually excited to build. My main design responsibility was the mechanics that affected bubble trajectory, which gave structure to the puzzles the other designer then built out.

Core design

Trajectory mechanics

The objects that redirect and change a bubble's path. These are the pieces every puzzle is built out of, so they had to be easy to read at a glance and predictable enough that players could plan around them.

Camera

Perspective swap toggle

A toggle letting players change viewing perspective, which mattered for reading bubble paths in a 3D space from a top-down view.

Environment

Floor tile generator and prop spawner

A randomized floor tile generator and a counter prop spawner, which added variety to levels without anyone having to hand-place decoration during a 96-hour deadline.

Systems

Early inventory prototype

A working inventory built before the final art assets existed, so the system could be tested and iterated while the art was still in progress.

Supporting the team

One of our programmers was completely new to Unity, so I spent a good amount of time helping him navigate the engine and troubleshoot problems as they came up. On a 96-hour project that isn't a distraction from the work, it is the work, because getting him unstuck was worth more than anything I would have gotten done taking his tasks myself.

Recognition

Published on Global Game Jam.

What I took from it

My first jam taught me that on a big team with a short deadline, the thing holding you back is almost never ideas. It's whether the work is getting integrated, whether everyone is aligned, and whether anyone is stuck.

Getting someone else's work into the build counts as your contribution too.
