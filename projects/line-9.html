Line 9

Overview

Line 9 was made during the 2025 GMTK Game Jam in 72 hours. It's a first-person 2.5D inspection game where dolls move along a conveyor belt that wraps around the player, and your job is to catch the ones with anomalies while approving the ones that are fine.

This was my second jam, on a remote team of six I had not worked with before aside from the other designer. Because we had fewer programmers than my previous jam, I took on considerably more hands-on implementation.

Goal
Build an inspection loop flexible enough to keep producing new anomalies and new ways of seeing them, without exceeding what six people can finish in three days.

The anomaly system

I designed and implemented the core anomaly system that drives the whole experience. Each round randomizes the doll's features, with specific traits flagged as either correct or faulty, so the player is comparing what they see against a mental model of what a correct doll looks like rather than memorizing a checklist.

On top of that I built a difficulty scaling system that makes anomalies subtler the more consistently the player catches them. The tension ramps up because the player is getting better, not because a script decided round three should be harder, and that difference is what keeps a jam game interesting past the first couple minutes.

Inspection stations

The inspection stations along the conveyor belt are where the systemic approach paid off. Each station uses layer masks to simulate a different viewing lens while reusing one shared underlying system, so building the second and third stations cost a fraction of what the first one did.

Station 01

UV lens

Reveals hidden glowing marks that are completely invisible under normal light, making some anomalies undetectable unless the player thinks to check.

Station 02

Magnifier

Shows a close-up of the doll's stitching, which catches defects too small to notice from normal viewing distance.

Station 03

X-ray

Shows the internal components, which is the only way to catch faults that leave nothing visible on the outside.

Architecture

One shared framework

All three stations are the same system set up with different layer masks, so a fourth or fifth station would have just been more content instead of more code if we had the time.

Working remote under a 72-hour clock

Like my previous jam we started with a group brainstorm where everyone pitched ideas, and I helped pull those into one direction the team felt good about. Doing that quickly matters even more remotely, since you can't read the room and a disagreement can quietly cost you six hours.

With a smaller asset count than BLOOPS, more of my time went directly into gameplay systems rather than art integration, though I still supported that where it was needed.

Recognition

Published on itch.io.

What I took from it

The station framework is a small version of how I like to work in general. One system, three different uses of it, built so that a fourth would have been easy.

With only 72 hours, building the flexible version was also the fast version.
