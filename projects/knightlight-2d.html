KnightLight

Overview

KnightLight was a two-semester, multidisciplinary project where programmers built a custom C++ engine while designers created the gameplay inside it. I served as Design Lead, owning player controller design, core mechanic design and implementation, prototyping, and playtesting. With no engine yet in semester one, I prototyped relentlessly in Unity — sometimes weekly — to keep playtests running while the engine came together.

When it became clear how much the engine required, I helped push a scope cut from a combat/metroidvania concept to a focused puzzle game built around light. That led to the Knight-and-Light split: the Light can leave the armor and move independently to solve light-based puzzles. I designed and built the Flash ability, the lamp/door progression system, checkpoint torches that doubled as recall points, the Signiflyer collectibles that guide players through the dark, the pipe traversal system, and most of the Steam achievements.

What I did

  • Served as Design Lead on a multidisciplinary two-semester project built on a custom C++ engine.
  • Created frequent Unity prototypes, sometimes weekly, to test gameplay concepts, player abilities, lighting interactions, and movement feel while the engine was still being built.
  • Helped scale the project down from a larger combat/metroidvania concept to a puzzle-focused game centered on light.
  • Redesigned the player into two interconnected parts, the Knight and the Light, creating a mechanic where the Light could move independently while the Knight remained behind.
  • Designed and implemented the Flash ability, lamp/door pairing progression system, and checkpoint torches that functioned as save points and recall points.
  • Iterated on visual and audio feedback after playtests showed players were confused about the armor recall system.
  • Created the Signiflyers collectible system, which doubled as subtle visual guidance in dark spaces.
  • Implemented most Steam achievements by building tracking hooks across multiple gameplay systems.
  • Built the pipe traversal system using directional tags, giving the level designer flexibility in puzzle construction.
  • Helped reinforce readability and atmosphere through lighting, particle work, and a proximity-based red glow on spike hazards.
  • Conducted regular playtests across both semesters and authored numerous detailed reports on player behavior, confusion points, and usability issues.
  • Co-authored an Engine Design Guide documenting workflows for future designers working in the custom engine.
  • Transitioned from Unity prototyping to direct implementation in the custom engine once it became stable.
