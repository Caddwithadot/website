Depth Strider

Overview

Depth Strider was my second DES365 Game Feel prototype, built solo over five weeks. The whole goal was a feeling: making the player feel like a deep-sea diver walking the ocean floor. It leans entirely on controller feel and environmental immersion rather than traditional mechanics or objectives.

I built a custom Cinemachine-driven controller with slow, heavy, resistant movement and delayed head rotation so the camera lags behind input, selling the weight of a bulky diving suit. A blinking distant ship guides the player forward, and post-processing, fog, floating particles, breathing bubbles, and early-prioritized audio complete the atmosphere.

What I did

  • Completed a five-week DES365 prototype focused on deep-sea diver movement feel and immersion.
  • Built a custom Unity player controller with Cinemachine for smooth look rotation, camera bob, and subtle screen shake.
  • Tuned movement to feel slow, heavy, and resistant, with gradual acceleration to simulate underwater movement.
  • Implemented delayed head rotation so the camera lagged slightly behind player input, reinforcing the feel of a bulky diving suit.
  • Added angle limits to the camera and helmet to keep the player view grounded and restricted.
  • Built an underwater ravine environment that naturally guided the player forward without explicit navigation systems.
  • Used a distant ship with blinking light as a persistent visual goal that grew larger as the player approached.
  • Added post-processing, fog, a custom skybox, floating particles, and breathing bubble effects to reinforce the underwater atmosphere.
  • Prioritized audio and ambience early, including underwater ambience and breathing sounds.
