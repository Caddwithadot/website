Forgeborne

Overview

Forgeborne was a five-week solo prototype for DigiPen's DES365 Game Feel course — a first-person action experience built around a single unifying mechanic: electromagnetism. The player uses metal-manipulating tech to forge and test adaptive weapons in a junkyard arena. The brief wanted three weapons with three attacks each; my goal was to tie all nine together through one systemic interaction rather than nine isolated kits.

To do that I built a runtime mesh-splitting system that breaks any assigned model into individual triangles to simulate shrapnel, plus a reform pipeline that flies, spins, and locks the pieces back into weapons. Every archetype reuses that same shrapnel system — the clearest example of a pattern I keep coming back to: build a flexible system first, then express many mechanics through it.

What I did

  • Completed a five-week solo prototype for DES365 Game Feel in Unity.
  • Built a first-person action experience centered on electromagnetism and adaptive weapon forging.
  • Created a runtime mesh-splitting system that broke assigned models into individual triangles to simulate metal shrapnel.
  • Built a reform animation pipeline that teleported, spun, and reassembled shrapnel into weapons, then dispersed it again during attacks.
  • Designed three weapon archetypes, each with three attack types, all tied into the same modular shrapnel system.
  • Pivoted away from a handcrafted primitive-shape approach when it became clear it would not scale within the five-week timeline.
  • Focused iteration on stabilizing and refining the electromagnetism system.
  • Learned that my strongest work tends to come from building flexible systems first and expressing them through multiple mechanics.
