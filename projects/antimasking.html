AntiMasking

Overview

AntiMasking was made during the 2026 Global Game Jam in 48 hours. It's a chaotic first-person auto-runner where you play a deranged individual sprinting through a grocery store ripping masks off shoppers. The team was ten people, including a few I had previously worked with on BLOOPS.

I could not dedicate as much time to this one as I would have liked, so my role was smaller than on previous jams. Where I did contribute most was at the front, in the brainstorming session that decided what we were actually going to build.

Goal
Get ten people with wildly different ideas pointed at one concept that could actually be finished in two days.

Scoping the concept

A lot of wild ideas got thrown around in that first session, and my focus was making sure whatever direction we picked was at least somewhat coherent and technically achievable inside 48 hours. Most of that time went into connecting ideas together, pointing out potential design and scope issues, and narrowing things toward something we could realistically execute.

Given how chaotic the concept we shipped is, it's worth saying that the ideas we cut were considerably more over the top.

Implementation support

Level

Whitebox troubleshooting

Helped work through issues with the level whiteboxing so the running route through the store held together.

Movement

Turning triggers

Adjusted all of the triggers that controlled how the auto-running character changed direction at corners, which is a small thing that makes the difference between an auto-runner feeling intentional and feeling broken.

Alignment

Documentation upkeep

Light documentation updates adding references and clarifying information, keeping ten people working from the same understanding under a short clock.

Concept

Scope flagging

Calling out design and scope risks early in the session, when changing direction is still cheap.

Recognition

Published on Global Game Jam.

What I took from it

Contributing well when you can't be there for the whole thing is its own problem. The most important hours on a ten-person jam are the first few, and being useful during those was worth more than spreading myself thin across the whole weekend.

Helping land on a concept that actually fits the deadline still counts, even when other people build it.
